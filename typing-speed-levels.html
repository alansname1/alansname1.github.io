What Are Typing Speed Levels?

Typing speed levels are broad categories used to describe how developed a person's keyboard skills may be.

They are usually based on words per minute, or WPM, but WPM alone does not tell the full story.

Your typing level also depends on:

  • Accuracy
  • Consistency
  • How long you can maintain your speed
  • How difficult the text is
  • How comfortably you type
  • Whether you can handle punctuation and numbers
Important: typing levels are useful reference points, not official grades or universal standards.

Typing Speed Levels at a Glance

WPM Range General Level Typical Focus
Under 20 WPM Early learner Learning key locations and basic keyboard control.
20–30 WPM Beginner Building familiarity and reducing keyboard searching.
30–40 WPM Developing Improving rhythm, accuracy, and sentence typing.
40–50 WPM Practical Building consistent everyday typing performance.
50–60 WPM Good Improving longer-session control and reducing errors.
60–80 WPM Strong Building speed while maintaining accuracy and endurance.
80–100 WPM Very fast Refining difficult words, punctuation, and consistency.
100+ WPM Exceptional speed Maintaining very high speed accurately over different types of text.

Level 1: Early Learner

An early learner may type below 20 WPM while becoming familiar with the keyboard.

At this stage, it is common to:

  • Search visually for keys
  • Use only a few fingers
  • Pause between letters or words
  • Make frequent corrections
  • Feel uncomfortable during longer typing sessions

The best priority is learning keyboard layout and building accurate movements.

Start with the Typing for Beginners Guide.

Level 2: Beginner

Around 20 to 30 WPM often represents a beginner who is developing more familiarity with the keyboard.

Useful goals at this stage include:

  • Use more fingers consistently.
  • Reduce repeated mistakes.
  • Learn the home row.
  • Look at the keyboard less often.
  • Practice complete words instead of isolated letters only.

The Keyboard Finger Placement Guide can help build better technique.

Level 3: Developing Typist

Around 30 to 40 WPM may indicate that basic typing skills are becoming more practical.

At this stage, you may already know most key locations but still pause during difficult words or punctuation.

A useful focus is:

  • Sentence rhythm
  • Capitalization
  • Punctuation
  • Common word patterns
  • Reducing corrections

Use Typing Practice Paragraphs to move beyond short word drills.

Level 4: Practical Typist

Around 40 to 50 WPM can be practical for many everyday tasks.

This may be comfortable for:

School

Assignments, notes, essays, and online learning.

Office Tasks

Email, forms, basic reports, and documents.

Communication

Chat messages, support replies, and online collaboration.

Everyday Use

Searches, messages, forms, and general computer work.

At this level, improving accuracy and consistency can often be more useful than chasing a much higher personal-best score.

Level 5: Good Typist

Around 50 to 60 WPM can represent solid practical keyboard ability for many users.

You may already type comfortably during everyday work.

To continue improving:

  • Use longer practice passages.
  • Work on difficult vocabulary.
  • Practice numbers and punctuation.
  • Reduce repeated errors.
  • Track accuracy over several tests.

A 5 Minute Typing Test can help measure whether your speed remains stable beyond a quick test.

Level 6: Strong Typist

Typing around 60 to 80 WPM is fast enough for many writing-heavy and computer-based tasks.

At this level, improvement may depend more on efficiency than simply trying to move your fingers faster.

Practice:

  • Difficult word combinations
  • Longer paragraphs
  • Punctuation
  • Numbers and symbols
  • Consistent rhythm
  • Maintaining accuracy while increasing pace

Level 7: Very Fast Typist

Around 80 to 100 WPM is very fast for many computer users.

At this stage, your biggest challenge may be maintaining performance rather than achieving a higher short-term burst.

Ask:

  • Can I maintain this speed for five minutes?
  • Does accuracy remain high?
  • Can I type unfamiliar text?
  • Can I handle punctuation smoothly?
  • Does my speed remain strong when the passage becomes difficult?

Level 8: Exceptional Speed

Typing above 100 WPM is very fast.

However, a high score should still be considered alongside accuracy and test conditions.

A 105 WPM score on a simple one-minute passage and a 105 WPM score maintained for ten minutes on complex text are not exactly the same achievement.

At very high speeds, useful measures include:

  • Consistency
  • Error rate
  • Long-test performance
  • Complex vocabulary
  • Punctuation and numbers

Why Accuracy Changes Your Typing Level

A raw WPM number can be misleading when mistakes are frequent.

Result What It Suggests
55 WPM with strong accuracy Controlled, practical typing.
70 WPM with many errors High raw speed but weaker effective performance.
65 WPM with stable accuracy over five minutes Strong consistency and useful real-world typing ability.
85 WPM for one minute but much slower over ten minutes Strong short-term speed with room to build endurance.

Read How to Increase Typing Accuracy for more help.

Why Test Length Matters

A typing level should not always be based on a single short test.

Test Length What It Shows
1 minute Quick speed and short-term rhythm.
5 minutes Consistency and sustained accuracy.
10 minutes Endurance, concentration, and long-form performance.

Try more than one test length before deciding what your normal typing level is.

How to Move Up a Typing Level

Instead of focusing only on a target WPM number, improve the skill that is currently limiting you.

Problem Best Practice Focus
You search for keys Finger placement and touch typing.
You make many mistakes Accuracy drills.
Your speed is inconsistent Longer typing passages.
Punctuation slows you down Sentence and paragraph exercises.
You type quickly only for short periods 5-minute and 10-minute practice.
You have stopped improving Review repeated errors and difficult word patterns.

Do You Need to Reach the Highest Level?

No.

The most useful typing level depends on what you actually need to do.

For example, someone who types accurately at 50 WPM may already have more than enough speed for many school, email, administrative, and everyday computer tasks.

Typing improvement should support your goals rather than becoming a race toward the highest possible number.

Set a Better Typing Goal

Instead of saying:

“I need to reach 80 WPM.”

Try a more useful goal:

“I want to type slightly faster while keeping my accuracy stable.”

Or:

“I want to maintain my normal WPM for five minutes without a large drop.”

These goals connect speed with real typing quality.

How to Measure Your Current Level

  1. Take a 1 Minute Typing Test.
  2. Record your WPM and accuracy.
  3. Take a 5 Minute Typing Test.
  4. Compare the two results.
  5. Notice repeated mistakes.
  6. Repeat the tests after several practice sessions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are typing speed levels?

Typing speed levels are broad WPM ranges used to describe stages of keyboard skill, from early learning to very fast typing.

What is a good typing level?

It depends on your needs. Around 40 to 60 WPM can be practical for many everyday school and office tasks when accuracy is strong.

Is 80 WPM advanced?

Around 80 WPM is a fast typing speed for many users, especially when it can be maintained with strong accuracy.

Does accuracy affect my typing level?

Yes. A lower WPM with strong accuracy can be more useful than a higher score with frequent errors and corrections.

Can my typing level change between tests?

Yes. Results can vary because of passage difficulty, fatigue, test length, concentration, and other factors. Use several results rather than one test alone.

How can I move to a higher typing level?

Practice consistently, improve repeated mistakes, use longer passages, and increase speed gradually while trying to keep accuracy stable.
