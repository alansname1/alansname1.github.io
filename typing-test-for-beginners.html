Where Should a Beginner Start?

If you are new to typing practice, you do not need to worry about reaching a particular WPM immediately.

A useful first goal is simply to become more comfortable with the keyboard while making fewer mistakes.

Start with the 1 Minute Typing Test. It is short enough to provide a baseline without requiring a long session.

Beginner rule: Use your first score as a starting point, not as a judgment. The purpose is to discover what you need to practice next.

What Should Beginners Measure?

WPM

Words per minute gives you a simple measurement of typing pace. Track it over time instead of worrying about one result.

Accuracy

Accuracy helps you see how cleanly you are typing and whether rushing is creating extra mistakes.

Keyboard Watching

Notice how often you look down. Reducing keyboard watching gradually can help build key-location memory.

Recurring Mistakes

Repeated errors show which letters, words, or movements deserve focused practice.

There Is No Required Beginner WPM

Beginners start at very different levels.

One person may already use a keyboard regularly but never learned touch typing. Another may be completely new to keyboard use. Comparing those two people by one WPM benchmark would not be very useful.

Instead, use stages of development:

Stage Main Focus
New to the keyboard Learn key locations and type short words accurately.
Building confidence Reduce hesitation and use more consistent finger movements.
Typing simple sentences Improve spacing, capitalization, punctuation, and rhythm.
Comfortable with short tests Increase pace gradually while maintaining accuracy.
Ready for longer tests Practice consistency with five-minute sessions and longer paragraphs.

For more context about interpreting WPM, read What Is a Good Typing Speed?.

A Simple Beginner Typing Routine

A practice session does not need to be complicated.

10-Minute Beginner Session

  1. Spend 2 minutes typing easy words slowly.
  2. Spend 3 minutes practicing simple sentences.
  3. Spend 2 minutes working on a difficult key or repeated mistake.
  4. Take one 1-minute typing test.
  5. Spend the final 2 minutes reviewing what went wrong.

The exact times do not matter as much as giving each part of the session a clear purpose.

Accuracy Before Maximum Speed

Beginners often try to increase WPM by moving their fingers as quickly as possible.

That can create a cycle:

  1. You rush.
  2. You make more errors.
  3. You use Backspace repeatedly.
  4. Your rhythm breaks.
  5. You become frustrated and rush again.

A better approach is to type at a pace where your movements feel controlled.

As those movements become more familiar, increase speed gradually.

Use Typing Accuracy Practice when mistakes are your main challenge.

Learn Where Your Fingers Belong

Consistent finger placement can reduce unnecessary hand movement.

The traditional home-row starting position is:

  • Left hand: A, S, D, F
  • Right hand: J, K, L, ;

The F and J keys usually have raised marks that can help you locate the home row without looking down.

You do not need to master every key immediately.

Learn more in Keyboard Finger Placement.

Is Looking at the Keyboard Bad?

Looking at the keyboard is normal when you are learning.

The goal is not to force yourself to stop looking immediately. Instead, reduce it gradually.

Try this:

  1. Type an easy sentence while watching the screen.
  2. If you lose your position, use the F and J key markers to reset your hands.
  3. Look down briefly only when necessary.
  4. Repeat the sentence.

Over time, frequently used keys should begin to feel more familiar.

Common Beginner Mistakes

Mistake Better Approach
Trying to type as fast as possible Use a controlled pace and build speed gradually.
Watching only WPM Track accuracy and repeated errors too.
Taking tests repeatedly without practice Work on one weakness between tests.
Getting discouraged by mistakes Use errors to identify what needs practice.
Practicing for too long at once Use shorter focused sessions that are easier to repeat.
Comparing yourself constantly with fast typists Compare your current results with your own earlier results.

When Should a Beginner Try the 10 Minute Test?

There is no need to rush into a long test.

Consider the 10 Minute Typing Test when:

  • One-minute tests feel comfortable.
  • You can type paragraphs without becoming frustrated.
  • You want to measure endurance.
  • You want to compare short-test and long-test performance.

Longer is not automatically better. Choose the test that matches your current goal.

How to Know You Are Improving

Improvement is not limited to WPM.

Look for:

  • Fewer mistakes
  • Less hesitation
  • Less keyboard watching
  • Smoother rhythm
  • More comfortable hand movement
  • Higher WPM without losing accuracy
  • Better consistency across several tests

Beginner Learning Path

A simple learning sequence can look like this:

  1. Learn the basic keyboard layout.
  2. Practice consistent finger placement.
  3. Type short words accurately.
  4. Move to simple sentences.
  5. Use short timed tests.
  6. Practice recurring mistakes.
  7. Add paragraph practice.
  8. Try longer tests when ready.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which typing test should a beginner start with?

A one-minute typing test is a practical starting point because it is short and easy to repeat. Longer tests can be added later.

Should beginners focus on WPM or accuracy?

Accuracy and controlled movement provide a useful foundation. Increase speed gradually as your keyboard familiarity improves.

Is it normal to look at the keyboard?

Yes. Keyboard watching is common while learning. Try to reduce it gradually rather than forcing yourself to stop immediately.

How often should beginners practice?

Regular short sessions are usually easier to maintain than occasional long sessions. Focus on consistency instead of trying to practice for hours.

Why does my typing score change every time?

Scores can vary because of concentration, passage difficulty, mistakes, hesitation, or simply having a better or worse attempt. Compare several sessions rather than relying on one score.

When should I try a longer typing test?

Try longer tests after short sessions feel comfortable and you want to measure consistency or endurance.
